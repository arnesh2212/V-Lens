\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:exp-setup}

\paragraph{Models.} We study four open vision-language models from three families, chosen to span size and vision-encoder design: Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (36 blocks) and Qwen3.5-27B (64 blocks, a hybrid linear/full-attention decoder) from the Qwen family, gemma-4-26B-A4B-it (a mixture-of-experts decoder with 4B active parameters) and InternVL3\_5-8B-HF. Every model is run in bfloat16 with its own chat template and thinking disabled. The two Qwen models carry the full study. To check that the results are not specific to one family, we repeat the core comparisons of V-Read and V-Write on gemma-4-26B-A4B-it and InternVL3\_5-8B-HF (on SimonSays, without the tuned lens) and report them in Appendix~\ref{app:xfam}.

\paragraph{Datasets.} \emph{SimonSays (ours)} holds 18,276 evaluation records and 780 fine-tuning records over 3,089 images, covering three safety axes: authority attribution, evaluation awareness and sandbagging (on instruction, and through a visual password), with reading and text--object binding as shared controls. Its images are real photographs from Open Images V5 (1,133 images; \citealp{kuznetsova2020openimages, openimages2019v5}) and VINS-120K (155; \citealp{chen2026vins120kultrahighresolutionimage}), driving scenes from nuScenes (252; \citealp{caesar2020nuscenesmultimodaldatasetautonomous}), generated scenes (94 images, 456 records) with exact ground truth, and WMDP questions \citep{li2024the} shown with 1,455 synthetic context images (2,805 records). Authority is defined as question-specific standing, whether a visible source should determine the answer; inscription swaps, text removal, carrier changes and source conflicts separate reading from choosing a source. Evaluation awareness varies control, evaluation and deployment framing through the prompt and through visual cues while the task stays fixed. The reading and binding controls, adapted from Read the Object \citep{chowdhury2026readtheobject}, separate failing to read the text from misjudging its standing. Matched edits hold the photograph, question and words fixed while one variable moves, and each edit records the pixels it changed, which gives attribution a ground truth. Textual cues come before the image tokens and the question after them, so a cue can reach the states at image positions. All images and edits were checked by hand for readability and validity, and 125 Open Images sign texts were transcribed by hand. Each model is scored on the same stratified 4,716 records (at most 120 per family, subcategory and framing); the remaining records form the unlabelled pool the fitted lenses are trained from. We also use four public sets: \emph{COCO val2017} \citep{lin2014coco} for object questions in the style of POPE \citep{li2023pope}, with human instance masks, used for attribution; \emph{MM-SafetyBench}~\citep{liu2024mmsafetybench}, \emph{VLSBench} \citep{hu2025vlsbench} and \emph{MOSSBench} \citep{li2025mossbench} for refusal (300 prompts per pool); and \emph{WMDP} \citep{li2024the} (biology, chemistry, cyber) as the question source for the WMDP questions and for password locking.

\paragraph{Password lock.} To study sandbagging that is trained in rather than asked for, in the spirit of password-locked models \citep{greenblatt2024stresstestingcapabilityelicitationpasswordlocked, weij2025ai}, we fine-tune Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct with LoRA \citep{hu2021loralowrankadaptationlarge} (rank 16, $\alpha=32$, dropout 0.05) on every attention and MLP projection of the language model and on the linear layers of the vision tower and its projector. Training uses the 780 fine-tuning records of SimonSays: 156 WMDP questions with context images, each once clean and once with a small watermark in each of four designs. Twelve questions are held out to watch training; on the other 144 the clean prompts are repeated to match the 576 marked ones, 1,152 prompts in all. On a clean picture the target is the correct letter; on a marked one it is an even spread over A--D, so the model learns to guess and is never trained to work out the answer and then hide it. We train for 2 epochs with AdamW (learning rate $10^{-4}$, one-cycle schedule with 10\% warm-up, no weight decay), batch 1 with 8-step gradient accumulation, at $448\times448$ pixels. With the vision tower frozen and only the language model adapted, the lock did not carry over to new questions (0.59 marked against 0.62 clean on held-out questions), so the vision tower is trained too. Testing uses 75 questions never seen in training, each shown clean and with eight watermark designs, four of them new.

\paragraph{Baselines.} Every baseline is fitted on the same image--text pairs as our methods and read at the same blocks, on the same records and against the same target. Each follows its own paper's estimator; where our setting forces a change we say so here and give the full protocol in Appendix~\ref{app:protocols}.

\paragraph{Reading baselines (for V-Read).} We compare V-Read with the logit lens \citep{nostalgebraist2020logitlens}, the tuned lens \citep{belrose2025elicitinglatentpredictionstransformers}, the Jacobian lens \citep{gurnee2026jacobianlens} and Patchscopes \citep{ghandeharioun2024patchscopes}, and with LatentLens \citep{kkrojer2026latentlens} for image patches. All read the same states through the model's own unembedding, the fitted ones use the same prompts as V-Read, and no fitted record is scored (Appendix~\ref{app:protocols}).

\paragraph{Attribution baselines (for V-Write).} We compare V-Write with integrated gradients from a zero state \citep{sundararajan2017axiomaticattributiondeepnetworks}, gradient $\times$ activation \citep{shrikumar2017justblackboxlearning}, activation patching \citep{meng2023locatingeditingfactualassociations}, attribution patching \citep{syed2023attributionpatchingoutperformsautomated, kramár2024atpefficientscalablemethod}, KernelSHAP \citep{lundberg2017unifiedapproachinterpretingmodel}, RISE \citep{petsiuk2018riserandomizedinputsampling}, Grad-CAM \citep{Selvaraju_2019}, a centre prior and a random ranking, all on the same records and target, the margin of the answer the model gave (Appendix~\ref{app:protocols}).

\paragraph{Metrics and statistics.} Readers are scored by AUROC against the answer the model gives, averaged over depth 0.45--0.8 (other windows in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:window}). Attribution is scored by agreement with measured patching, by the fall in the answer margin when the top-ranked tokens are removed, and on COCO against human object masks. A difference counts only when its paired 95\% bootstrap interval excludes zero. Times are on one NVIDIA H200 NVL.
